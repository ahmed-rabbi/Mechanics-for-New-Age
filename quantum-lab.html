Quantum Laboratory

LAB 07 — Exploring Atomic and Energy Concepts

1. Introduction

The M4NA Quantum Laboratory is a dedicated research space for examining proposed relationships involving applied force, energy, electrons and atomic structures.

This laboratory will progressively present the concepts and research propositions developed within the Mechanics for New Age framework.

Research direction: Examine atomic and energy-related phenomena through the conceptual approach of M4NA, with attention to the proposed role of applied force and energy.

2. M4NA Atomic and Energy Research

CONCEPT 01

Applied Force and Energy

Explore the M4NA proposal that applied force converts into energy, and examine the possible relationship between force activation and energy-related processes.

Research Concept
CONCEPT 02

Electron Energy Capture

Explore the M4NA proposal that electrons capture energy and may consequently make greater-distance jumps or involve a greater number of electrons in transitions.

Research Concept
CONCEPT 03

Atomic Breakdown

Examine the M4NA proposal that atomic breakdown yields energy together with residue.

Research Concept

3. Research Areas

The laboratory will provide a structured place to develop, examine and document the following areas:

  • Applied force and energy conversion.
  • Energy capture by electrons.
  • Electron transitions and energy-related changes.
  • Atomic breakdown and its resulting products.
  • Further M4NA investigations into atomic structures.
These are research propositions within the M4NA framework. Their scientific validity and physical implications require appropriate theoretical development and experimental investigation.

4. Reserved Research Concept

Reserved Topic

Nuclear Blanket

This research topic is reserved for a future presentation within the M4NA framework.

5. Research Philosophy

The M4NA Quantum Laboratory is intended to develop a systematic research space in which proposed relationships among force, energy and atomic phenomena can be presented and examined.

Each concept will be developed progressively, with its assumptions, mathematical formulation and supporting evidence considered separately.
